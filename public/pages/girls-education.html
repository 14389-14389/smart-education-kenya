Why Keeping Girls in School is a National Priority

When a girl is educated, she is more likely to earn a higher income, have a healthier family, and invest back into her community.

Girls' Dignity Project Published: Sep 30, 2024

Overview

Educating girls is one of the most effective strategies for national development. Yet, barriers such as period poverty, early marriage, and cultural norms keep many girls out of school. Our Girls' Dignity Project addresses these challenges by ensuring girls have the support and resources to stay in school and thrive.

Why this matters

  • Economic growth: Educated women contribute significantly to the national economy and reduce poverty rates.
  • Healthier families: Education leads to improved maternal and child health outcomes.
  • Social transformation: Educated girls are more likely to challenge harmful practices and advocate for change.
  • Generational impact: Girls who stay in school are more likely to educate their children, creating a lasting ripple effect.

Key interventions

  • Providing sanitary products and hygiene education
  • Community sensitization to end early marriage
  • Mentorship and leadership programs for girls
  • Scholarships and school support initiatives

Success story

In Kitui County, the Girls’ Dignity Project distributed reusable sanitary pads to over 1,000 girls. School attendance improved by 19%, and dropout rates significantly decreased. Teachers also received training to support girls during menstruation, creating a more inclusive environment.

"I no longer have to miss school because of my period. I want to be a nurse and help other girls too." — Faith, 15

How to support

  • Donate menstrual hygiene kits
  • Sponsor a girl's education
  • Support mentorship and leadership camps
  • Partner with schools to expand the program

Quick facts

  • 1 in 10 girls in Kenya miss school due to period poverty
  • Educated girls are 50% more likely to earn a living wage
  • Girls' education contributes to lower child mortality rates
